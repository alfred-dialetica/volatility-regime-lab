What the book is actually long and short

Conceptual · not a position recommendation
01 · Long

Single-name variance

Buy a weighted basket of constituent options or variance. Idiosyncratic moves, earnings and stock-specific shocks can help.

02 · Short

Index variance

Sell SPX variance or options against the basket. Common market moves make the index leg expensive when expected correlation is high.

03 · Residual

Short correlation

The relative book benefits when realized co-movement is lower than the correlation embedded at entry. Correlation spikes are the central crash risk.

Methodology

Observed measures first. Derived measures labelled.

This prototype uses public end-of-day series. It is designed to explain a volatility regime, not to produce a trading recommendation.

01 · Observed

Cboe indices

VIX1D, VIX9D, VIX, VIX3M, VIX6M and VIX1Y define the constant-maturity curve. VVIX and SKEW add vol-of-vol and tail-risk context.

02 · Observed

S&P 500 close

Daily S&P 500 levels come from FRED. The dashboard does not ingest private positions, order flow or paid option-chain data.

03 · Derived

Realized volatility

RV21, RV63 and RV252 are annualized standard deviations of daily log returns. The displayed volatility premium is backward-looking.

04 · Derived

Regime score

A transparent heuristic combines rolling percentiles for VIX, VVIX, SKEW and curve inversion. It is descriptive and intentionally not predictive.

05 · Observed

Dispersion and constituent vol

DSPX measures expected 30-day S&P 500 dispersion from SPX and selected single-stock options. VIXEQ represents the market-cap-weighted root-mean-square implied volatility of the eligible constituent basket.

06 · Observed

Implied correlation

COR1M, COR3M, COR6M and COR1Y estimate average expected correlation for the top 50 value-weighted SPX names using ATM index and component option implied volatilities.
